# Content patterns

What different kinds of content are for, and the placement/tagging principles that keep them findable. Choose a content type by what the reader needs to do, not by what the organization wants to publish.

## Content types

- **Service page** — the reader completes a task: apply, request, pay, register, report, renew, schedule. Prioritize task completion; surface requirements, cost, timing, and contact paths; keep it evergreen unless the underlying service changes.
- **Guide or explainer** — the reader needs to understand a process, rule, or decision before acting. Keep it task-oriented, link to the service page where the actual action happens, and avoid duplicating policy source text unless necessary.
- **Policy or official requirement** — the content itself is the authoritative rule. Identify the source of authority, include version/effective date/owner/review cycle, and provide a plain-language summary separately when the policy text itself can't be simplified.
- **News or announcement** — timeliness is the main value. Never use news as the permanent source of truth for a service or policy; link to the evergreen page that gets updated separately, and define when the item goes stale.
- **Alert or urgent notice** — the reader needs immediate, time-sensitive information (disruption, emergency, closure, deadline change, safety notice). State who's affected and what to do now, with a clear start/end time and an automatic removal trigger.
- **Event** — the reader attends, registers for, or needs to know about a scheduled occurrence. Include date, time, location, registration, and accessibility accommodations contact; archive or convert the page after the event.
- **Campaign or landing page** — a temporary or thematic entry point across multiple pieces of content. Define the campaign window and an unpublish/archive/conversion plan *before* launch; link to permanent source pages instead of duplicating evergreen content.
- **Form, document, or downloadable asset** — the file itself is necessary for a transaction, record, or offline workflow. Don't use a downloadable file as a substitute for a web page when the content is meant to be read online (see `marin-digital-standards/accessibility/documents.md`). Assign a source of truth and a version-replacement process.
- **Directory or index** — the reader needs to find people, services, locations, or related content. Define inclusion criteria, use consistent metadata, and remove stale entries.

## Page purpose types

Every page's primary purpose falls into roughly one of these shapes, each with its own required elements:

- **Transactional** (apply, pay, renew, report, register): eligibility, requirements, steps, fees, deadlines, processing time, confirmation/next step.
- **Informational** (understand a topic or decision): a clear answer to the primary question, scope and limits, related actions, source of truth.
- **Navigational** (choose where to go next): distinct pathways, descriptive link labels, clear criteria for choosing a path.
- **Participatory** (engage in a public process): who can participate, how, dates/deadlines, what the input affects, where outcomes get published.
- **Accountability or transparency** (documents decisions, budgets, records, performance): authority/source, date/version, owner, update cadence.

State a page's purpose as: *"This page exists to help [audience] [complete a task or answer a question] so they can [outcome]."* See `principles.md` for why this framing matters.

## Placement and tagging

- Prefer labels the reader actually recognizes over internal program or department jargon.
- Categories don't need to be perfectly mutually exclusive, but each one should help the reader make a meaningful distinction — a category that never changes anyone's path isn't earning its place.
- Apply the smallest useful set of tags; over-tagging reduces findability as much as under-tagging does.
- Department ownership and reader-facing topic are not the same axis — capture both only when both are genuinely useful to someone using the site, not by default.
