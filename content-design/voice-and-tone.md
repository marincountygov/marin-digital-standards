# Voice and tone

## Voice

- Use active voice when it makes clear who does what. Ask: who does what? *"The form must be submitted by June 1"* (passive, actor missing) → *"You must submit the form by June 1"* (active, actor clear).
- Use passive voice only when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant, when naming an actor would misassign responsibility, or when the law itself triggers the result without an actor.
- Address the reader as "you" when appropriate; use "we" for the agency or organization when responsibility matters and is clear.
- Use present tense where possible.

## Tone

Be direct and respectful. Don't make content childish, patronizing, or artificially casual — match the seriousness of the subject. A notice about a fee waiver and a notice about an emergency evacuation both need to be clear, but they don't sound the same.

Use examples to clarify abstract rules, when a reader must choose between options, when a condition or exception is easy to misread, or when a calculation or document requirement needs illustration.

## Sensitive contexts

When content involves violence, disaster, loss, enforcement, illness, disability, benefits, or housing instability, tone carries more weight than usual — see `inclusive-language.md` for the specific guidance on trauma-informed framing, avoiding blame, and avoiding unnecessary graphic detail.
