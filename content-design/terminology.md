# Terminology

## Use one term per concept

Don't alternate between *resident, applicant, customer, participant, beneficiary,* and *client* for the same person unless those categories are meaningfully different in context. Picking one term and using it consistently throughout a piece of content, and ideally across related content, reduces the reader's cognitive load — an alternating term reads as though it might mean something different each time.

## Requirement words have defined meaning

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| must | required |
| must not | prohibited |
| may | permitted |
| should | recommended, not required |
| will | future action or expected result |

Avoid "shall" as a general-purpose requirement word in public-facing content — convert it based on what it actually means: *"The applicant shall submit..."* → "You must submit..."; *"The department shall review..."* → "We will review..."; *"The permit shall expire..."* → "The permit expires..." Keep "shall" only when quoting required legal text verbatim.

## Common plain-language replacements

See `plain-language.md` for the full word-choice list. The short version: prefer the common word over the formal one (*use* over *utilize*, *help* over *facilitate*, *before* over *prior to*) unless the audience specifically needs the formal or technical term.

## Terms requiring care

See `inclusive-language.md` for terminology involving disability, gender/family, trauma, age/income, race/ethnicity/national origin, and public safety/health — those are governed by that standard, not this one, because word choice there carries risk beyond plain clarity.
