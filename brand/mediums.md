# Mediums

How County identity (`identity.md`, `logo.md`, `color.md`, `typography.md`) applies across specific communication channels.

## General principle

Start from an official County or department template when one exists. Preserve official logo placement and proportions; apply color and type guidance consistently. Never invent campaign marks, lockups, seals, taglines, or department logos to fill a gap — flag the gap instead.

## Email signatures

Follow the Identity Style Guide's email signature treatment when available. Keep name, title, department, phone, email, and web information clean and scannable. Avoid inspirational quotes, decorative images, excess icons, and unrelated badges. Don't default to a large image-only signature block.

## Presentations

Use an official County or department deck when available. Place identity consistently — usually a footer, title slide, or end slide, as the template directs. Use one visual hierarchy for titles, section dividers, body copy, captions, and source notes. Keep slides sparse; avoid decorative transitions, heavy shadows, clip art, and unnecessary gradients.

## Reports, one-pagers, and flyers

Include a clear title, date, department/program owner, and contact or next-step information. Use consistent heading levels and spacing. Use tables, callouts, and sidebars only when they improve scanning, not as decoration. Keep identity visible but not oversized — don't crowd the page with competing logos.

## Social media

Use the approved identity treatment for avatars/profile graphics. Keep post text short and visually clear, sized for mobile viewing. Use official department or campaign marks only when supplied — don't recreate the logo inside post artwork when the platform avatar already carries identity, unless a template calls for it.

## Video

Place logo identity on opening cards, lower thirds, or end cards as the guide or template directs, with clear space and minimum readable size preserved. Keep lower thirds consistent across a series. Never animate the official logo in a way that distorts, breaks apart, recolors, or trivializes it.

## Forms and reusable layouts

Use consistent field labels, section headings, spacing, and instructions. Keep County/department identity visible without over-branding a routine administrative form. Don't create a competing visual system inside a single form — one set of visual states, labels, and helper text across the whole set.
