# Forms and transactions

The service-design layer of a form, application, payment, upload, signature, or registration — what it needs to state and do so residents can start, complete, submit, and recover from it. For the *wording* of forms and errors, see `marin-digital-standards/content-design/forms-and-errors.md`; for the *structural/accessibility* requirements, see `marin-digital-standards/accessibility/forms.md`. This document is the third angle: what the transaction itself needs to account for.

## Core rules

- Explain who should use the form before asking for information.
- State estimated completion time, required documents, fees, and deadlines *before* the form starts.
- Ask only for information needed to complete the service — every extra field costs every single person who fills it out.
- Group related fields together; label them using terms residents understand.
- Explain why sensitive or unusual information is needed.
- Preserve the resident's work when errors occur, wherever technically possible.
- Provide a confirmation, receipt, case number, or next-step message after submission — see `errors-and-recovery.md`.

## Before the resident starts, state

Who can use this form or transaction · what it does and doesn't do · what information and documents are required · accepted file types and size limits, if uploads are involved · fees and accepted payment methods · the deadline or service window · estimated completion time · whether the resident can save and return · what confirmation they'll receive.

## Payment flows

State: the amount or how it's calculated · due date · accepted payment methods · processing fees, if any · refund policy or non-refundable status · what happens after payment · what proof of payment the resident receives · what to do if payment fails or is charged incorrectly.

## Uploads and attachments

State: exact document names or acceptable alternatives · accepted file types and size limits · whether photos are accepted · whether originals, scans, or copies are required · what happens if documents are missing or unreadable.

## Field-level review

For each field or question: is this information actually required for the service outcome? Does the resident know the answer offhand? Is the label clear without internal jargon? Is the expected format obvious? Is help text needed? Is it placed near related fields? Could it be prefilled or avoided entirely? What happens if the resident leaves it blank?

## What makes a form/transaction ready

Residents understand whether to use it, what they need, how long it takes, how to complete it, what proof they receive, and how to recover if something goes wrong.
