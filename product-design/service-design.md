# Service design

The resident's end-to-end journey through a service — not just the page or form currently in front of them.

## Journey stages

A resident's experience of a service, whether or not the County's own systems recognize these as distinct stages:

1. **Recognize need** — the resident realizes they need to apply, pay, report, request, register, appeal, contact, or check eligibility.
2. **Find service** — search, navigation, a link, a notice, or contacting staff.
3. **Evaluate fit** — is this the right service, and am I eligible?
4. **Prepare** — gather documents, information, account access, a payment method, or authorization.
5. **Start** — open the form, call, email, visit, sign in, or begin the transaction.
6. **Complete** — submit information, pay, schedule, sign, upload, or confirm.
7. **Receive confirmation** — a receipt, case number, tracking number, email, or staff confirmation.
8. **Wait or act** — wait for review, respond to requests, attend an appointment, or complete additional steps.
9. **Resolve** — approved, denied, fulfilled, closed, renewed, escalated, appealed, or withdrawn.
10. **Recover** — handle an error, missing item, denial, missed deadline, payment problem, or changed circumstance.

A service designed around only stages 5–7 (the transaction itself) while ignoring 1–4 and 8–10 will feel broken to residents even when the transaction itself works perfectly.

## What a journey needs to define

Resident goal, resident situation/trigger, service outcome, channels involved, County teams/systems involved, required resident actions, required County actions, hand-off points, the completion point, and failure/recovery paths.

## Eligibility and requirements

Distinguish these explicitly — they're different things and get confused when they aren't:

- **Eligibility** — who may use the service or qualify for the outcome.
- **Requirements** — what the resident must provide, do, pay, prove, sign, or complete.
- **Conditions** — facts that change the resident's path: location, income, age, property type, deadline, prior approval, household status.
- **Disqualifiers** — facts that mean the resident should not start this service, or should use a different one.

Put eligibility and requirements *before* the start action, not discovered partway through a form. State disqualifying conditions clearly, with an alternative path — don't just say no. Write conditional requirements as explicit "if" statements. State whether staff verify eligibility after submission, so the resident knows whether self-checking is sufficient.
