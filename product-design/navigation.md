# Navigation

Findability, wayfinding, and site structure — helping a resident get from "I have a need" to "I found the right page" and back out again.

## The four questions navigation must answer

1. Am I in the right place?
2. Is this the page or path I need?
3. What related information should I consider next?
4. What should I do if this page is not the right one?

## Core principle

Prioritize the user's mental model over internal organizational structure. Department ownership matters for governance (see `marin-skills/content-governance`), but user-facing navigation should reflect how people look for services, tasks, topics, and life events — not the org chart.

## Decision rules

- **Navigation labels** — short, concrete, based on user language. Avoid department jargon, program names, acronyms, slogans, or anything requiring insider knowledge to parse.
- **Page placement** — content lives where its primary audience expects to find it, not necessarily where the owning department sits organizationally.
- **Cross-linking** — links reflect meaningful user journeys: to pages that help someone decide, act, recover, compare, or continue. Not an exhaustive list of everything tangentially related.
- **Search snippets** — tell the user what the page is, who it's for, and what they can do there — distinct enough that two similar pages don't read identically in results.
- **Duplication and canonical pages** — never let multiple pages compete to answer the same user question. Pick one canonical page, consolidate overlapping content, and redirect or clearly demote the rest.
- **Topic taxonomy** — see `marin-digital-standards/content-design/content-patterns.md` for the shared taxonomy-design principles (user-centered labels, avoid over-tagging, separate departments from topics); this document doesn't restate them.

## What a good IA recommendation includes

User intent, the content object being placed/labeled/linked, its current home (if known), its recommended home and why, the likely navigation path from homepage/section landing/search/related page, its relationship to parent/sibling/child/canonical/duplicate pages, and a way to measure whether findability actually improved.
